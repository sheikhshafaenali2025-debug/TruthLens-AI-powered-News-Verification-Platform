Method

Six instruments, one reading.

TruthLens doesn't just guess real or fake — it shows its working, the way a fact-checker would annotate a printed page.

01 / Model

Language forensics

Trained on verified and disputed articles, the model reads sentence structure, sourcing cues, and word choice.

02 / Source

Publisher credibility

Cross-checks the named outlet, when given, against a table of known reliable and unreliable publishers.

03 / Claims

Claim extraction

Pulls out the specific, checkable statements in the piece — the ones worth verifying independently.

04 / Trail

Standing case file

Every reading, its keywords, and its verdict roll up into a running desk log for the session.

Procedure

What happens when you hit analyze.

01

Text intake

Your pasted article — and source, if given — is cleaned and broken into sentences and tokens.

02

Feature extraction

Keyword weight, sentence complexity, tone, and named claims are measured against the training set.

03

Model scoring

The classifier weighs each feature and produces a raw probability split between real and fake.

04

Cross-checks

Source credibility, bias signals, and comparable coverage from verified outlets are pulled in alongside the verdict.

05

Case filed

The full report is saved to your desk log, with room for you to agree, disagree, or flag it for review.

Reading the room
  • Check the byline and outlet before the headline — the source is the first fact.
  • Read past the first paragraph; misleading pieces often front-load their strongest claim.
  • Look for named sources and dates, not just quotes in the abstract.
  • A claim with no citation is a claim, not a fact — even if it sounds precise.
  • Cross-check the same story on one outlet you don't usually read.
